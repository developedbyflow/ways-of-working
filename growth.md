# Growth

- **Use it when:** after launch, you work on getting more of the right users and keeping them.
- **Not when:** you need proof for one change → `/wow-experiment`. Growth runs many of those.
- **Reads:** the funnel from the product review, `docs/tracking-plan.md`, the results in `docs/gtm/launch-plan.md`, `docs/gtm/positioning.md`.
- **Writes:** `docs/gtm/growth.md`: the funnel numbers, the channel tests, the experiment backlog and the results.

## Steps
1. **The funnel, one number per step:**
   - getting users;
   - their first value (activation);
   - coming back (retention);
   - telling others (referral);
   - paying (revenue).

   Fix the leakiest step that matters first, usually activation or retention, before buying more traffic.
   - Skip it → you pay for visitors who leave.
2. **Channels:**
   - list many: content and SEO, communities, YouTube and social, partnerships, paid ads, referrals, direct sales;
   - test three cheaply;
   - focus on the one that works.
   - Skip it → you spread thin over channels that never get a real test.
3. **An experiment backlog:** each item has a hypothesis, a metric and a RICE score. Run 1–2 at a time (`/wow-experiment`).
4. **Content, if it's a channel:**
   - each piece shows a real job done;
   - one piece gets reused across channels;
   - a pace you can keep, with no streak to protect.
5. **A weekly review:** the numbers, what you learned, the next experiment.
   - STOP: you pick the next one.

## Done when
- [ ] each funnel step has its number
- [ ] the channel focus comes from a test
- [ ] the experiment backlog exists
- [ ] the weekly review happens

## Concepts if you get stuck
- The AARRR funnel
- Choosing channels by testing several, then focusing on one
- RICE
