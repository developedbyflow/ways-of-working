# Customer interviews

- **Use it when:** you need to learn what people actually do and pay for, before building, or to understand the users of a product you already have.
- **What you get:** what people really do and pay for, instead of a polite "I'd use it".
- **Run it:** `/wow-interview gym beginners who track what they eat`
- **Not when:** you're testing whether people can use a screen → the usability test in `/wow-ui-design`.
- **Reads:** the riskiest assumption, the brief if there is one.
- **Writes:** `docs/interviews/YYYY-MM-DD-<role>.md` (one note per interview), `docs/opportunities.md`.

## Steps
1. **Who:** five people who have the problem now, not friends being nice. Find them in communities, among your users, or on LinkedIn.
   - Skip it → kind opinions from people who will never be customers.
2. **Ask about their past, not your idea:**
   - "Tell me about the last time you...", "What did you do about it?", "What does it cost you?", "What have you tried?"
   - Never "Would you use...?" or "Would you pay...?"
   - Skip it → compliments and "yes, I'd use it", which predict nothing.
3. **Run it:** 20–30 minutes, and they talk most of the time. Ask "why" and "tell me more". Record only with their permission, otherwise write notes right after.
4. **A note after each one:**
   - what they did (facts);
   - their pains, with their own words;
   - what they already pay for;
   - your interpretation, kept separate from the facts.
5. **After five:** look for patterns, and add the needs and pains to `docs/opportunities.md`.
   - STOP: does the evidence support the idea, change it, or kill it?
6. **Ask for something real:** an email for the beta, a pre-order, an intro to the person who decides.
   - Skip it → polite interest mistaken for demand.

Interview notes hold personal data: ask for consent, keep them private, no names in public repos.

## Done when
- [ ] five notes, with facts separated from interpretation
- [ ] `docs/opportunities.md` updated
- [ ] the decision about the idea is written down

## Concepts if you get stuck
- The Mom Test: questions about their past, not your idea
- Continuous discovery: interviews every week
- Opportunity solution tree

## Next level
- One interview every week, even when nothing is being decided.
- `staff` A shared note template, so the whole team learns from every interview.
