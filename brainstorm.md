# Brainstorm a new product idea

- **Use it when:** you have an idea for a new app or product, or you want to find one.
- **What you get:** 1–2 ideas picked by score, each with the riskiest assumption and a way to test it.
- **Run it:** `/wow-brainstorm an app for people who cook for the week on Sundays`
- **Not when:** the product already exists and you're looking for what to improve → `/wow-product-review`.
- **Reads:** your notes, what you already know about the problem.
- **Writes:** `docs/product/ideas.md`: the problem, the ideas, the 1–2 you picked and why.

## Steps
1. **Start from a problem, not a solution:** who has it, when it happens, what they do today. Write it as a job story: "When <situation>, <who> wants <outcome>, but <obstacle>."
   - Skip it → you build a solution looking for a problem.
2. **What they use today:** other apps, spreadsheets, paper, a person, or nothing. That is your real competition.
   - Skip it → "no competitors", which usually means no market, or that you didn't look.
3. **Many ideas, no judging:** at least 10 ways to solve it, including ones without software. Then turn the problem into questions: "How might we...?" Time box: 20–30 minutes.
4. **Group and score:** group similar ideas, then score each from 1 to 5 on:
   - how painful the problem is;
   - how often it happens;
   - whether you can reach those people;
   - whether you can build a first version alone in a few weeks.
   - STOP: you pick 1–2.
5. **The riskiest assumption** of each pick: the one that kills the idea if it's false. For example: people don't really have this problem, they won't pay, you can't reach them.
   - Skip it → months of building before you test what a week could have tested.
6. **Plan the test:** talk to people (`/wow-interview`), check the market (`/wow-market-research`), or put up a fake door (`/wow-experiment`).

## Done when
- [ ] the problem is written as a job story
- [ ] what people use today is listed
- [ ] 1–2 ideas are picked, with their scores
- [ ] each pick has its riskiest assumption and a test for it

## Concepts if you get stuck
- Jobs to be done, job stories
- Product discovery
- How might we
