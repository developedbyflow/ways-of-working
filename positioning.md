# Positioning

- **Use it when:** before a launch, a landing page or a price: you decide who the product is for, and why it's their best choice.
- **What you get:** a message your best-fit customers understand in five seconds.
- **Run it:** `/wow-positioning the meal planner`
- **Not when:** you're planning the launch itself → `/wow-launch`.
- **Reads:** `docs/product/brief.md`, the market research in `docs/research/`, the notes in `docs/interviews/`, what users say about your product.
- **Writes:** `docs/gtm/positioning.md`.

Do the steps in this order: each one depends on the one before it.

## Steps
1. **Competitive alternatives:** what your best customers would use if you didn't exist. A spreadsheet and "doing nothing" count.
   - Skip it → you compare yourself with what you think the competition is, not with what customers actually use.
2. **What only you have:** features, approach, expertise that the alternatives lack.
3. **The value:** what those things let customers do, with proof (numbers, quotes).
4. **Best-fit customers:** who cares most about that value. Name traits you can recognize, not only age or country.
5. **Market category:** the frame that makes your value obvious. "A meal planner" and "a nutrition tracker" set different expectations for the same app.
   - Skip it → people compare you to the wrong thing, and you lose.
6. **The message:** a one-liner, a headline and a subheadline, three value points, each with its proof.
   - STOP: show it to five best-fit people. Can they say what it is and who it's for after five seconds?

## Done when
- [ ] the five parts are written, in order
- [ ] the one-liner and the message are written
- [ ] five best-fit people understood it in five seconds

## Concepts if you get stuck
- Positioning: alternatives, unique attributes, value, best-fit customers, market category
- Value proposition
